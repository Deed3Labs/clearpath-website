01Structure

Two balance sheets that never touch.

Housing on one side: land and contributed property. Money on the other: deposits, credit and loans.

OwnsContractOwnerless MoneyHousing
SelectedThe co-op
What it isMembers, one member one vote. It owns everything in the tree except the foundation.
How outside capital enters
Members hold no shares in Clear Capital Holdings. It is a separate company with its own board. Land backstops construction borrowing and never credit losses. A member’s savings never fund another member’s credit.
  • The co-op, members, one member one vote. Owns: Program entity (accounts, cards, payments); Deed & Title Co (records, administration); Clear Capital Holdings (where outside investors sit), which owns Clear Lending (funds the credit book) and Clear Properties (contributed property); ClearLabs (builds the protocol).
  • Outside the ownership tree: the Protocol foundation, ownerless, holds the source, and contracts ClearLabs to maintain it.

03The protocol

Control is unnecessary when exit is credible.

The ledger, the credit issuers and the deed and title records run on open-source contracts held by a foundation nobody owns.

Who maintains itUnder contract
ClearLabs, and not only ClearLabs.

Paid to maintain the contracts today, and deliberately not the only party that could be.

If governance divergedA right and a capability
The co-op can fork the code.

It already funds the maintainer, so it has the capability and not just the right.
