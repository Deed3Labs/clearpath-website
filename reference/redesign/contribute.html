01How it works

A contribution, not a sale.

Four steps, and none of them is a closing.

01§721

02The downsides

The honest downsides.

Every one is a real cost of doing it this way.

Five costsRead before you sign
  1. 01LiquidityShares are illiquid compared with cash.
  2. 02ControlYou do not control the properties.
  3. 03PaperworkYou get a K-1, which arrives later than a 1099, and your accountant will charge for it.
  4. 04Deferred taxThe deferred gain still exists, and comes due if you ever sell the shares.
  5. 05ConcentrationRisk moves from your one building to a fund. Usually better, but a change, not a removal.
We would rather you read these here than discover them after you sign.
03Who this is for

Three people this is built for.

Each one is giving up something different, and getting the same thing back.

Tired landlords01
You are done with tenants.

Rentals, ideally paid off, and no appetite left for 3 a.m. calls. Selling triggers the tax all at once. Contributing does not.

People holding raw land02
Pooled, it becomes scale.

A parcel you meant to build on. Alone it is hard to sell in a thin market. Pooled, it can be financed and developed.

Homeowners with a backyard03
Your yard, earning.

A ground lease, a removable unit and monthly income. You approve decisions, and Clear handles permitting.

Backyards are covered in full on the housing page.How backyards work
04One thing worth saying out loud

A contribution is designed to be held.

If you are looking to trade property every couple of years, this is the wrong vehicle, and we will tell you that on the first call.

Why it has to be heldAnti-abuse rules

The rules that make the deferral work also make swapping in and out every couple of years unworkable.
